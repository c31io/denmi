Algebraic Topology

Definition

Algebraic topology associates algebraic invariants (groups, rings) to topological spaces to study their properties. ## Key Properties - The fundamental group \(\pi_1(X,x_0)\) classifies loops up to homotopy. - Covering spaces correspond to subgroups of \(\pi_1\). - Homology groups \(H_n(X)\) satisfy \(H_0(X)\cong \mathbb{Z}\) for path‑connected \(X\) and functoriality. - Brouwer fixed point theorem: any continuous \(f:D^n\to D^n\) has a fixed point. - Jordan curve theorem: any continuous embedding \(S^1\hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^2\) separates \(\mathbb{R}^2\) into two components. ## Main Theorem The Hurewicz theorem links \(\pi_1\) and \(H_1\): \(H_1(X)\cong \pi_1(X,x_0)^{\mathrm{ab}}\). Moreover, for \(n\ge2\), the Hurewicz isomorphism \(\pi_n(X,x_0)\cong H_n(X)\) holds when \(\pi_k(X,x_0)=0\) for \(k<n\). This allows computation of homology from the fundamental group.
