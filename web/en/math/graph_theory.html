Graph Theory

Definition

A simple graph \(G=(V,E)\) has vertex set \(V\) and edge set \(E\subseteq\binom{V}{2}\). Its adjacency matrix \(A\in\{0,1\}^{|V|\times|V|}\) satisfies \(A_{ij}=1\) iff \(\{i,j\}\in E\). ## Key Properties - \(d(v)=|\{e\in E: v\in e\}|\) is the degree of \(v\). - \(G\) is Eulerian (contains a closed trail using each edge once) iff \(G\) is connected and every vertex has even degree. - The Laplacian \(L=D-A\) (with \(D\) diagonal of degrees) has \(L\mathbf1=0\). - The chromatic polynomial \(P(G,k)\) counts proper \(k\)‑colorings of \(V\). - The Turán graph \(T_r(n)\) is the complete \(r\)‑partite graph with parts as equal as possible. ## Main Theorem For any simple \(G=(V,E)\) with \(n=|V|\) and Laplacian \(L\), the number \(\tau(G)\) of spanning trees equals any cofactor of \(L\) (Kirchhoff’s Matrix‑Tree Theorem). Moreover, - \(G\) has an Eulerian circuit \(\iff\) every vertex has even degree. - \(P(G,k)\) is a polynomial in \(k\) of degree \(n\) with leading coefficient \(1\). - The extremal number \(\operatorname{ex}(n,K_{r+1})\) (maximum edges without a \((r+1)\)‑clique) satisfies \[\operatorname{ex}(n,K_{r+1}) = \Bigl(1-\frac1r\Bigr)\frac{n^2}{2},\] attained uniquely by \(T_r(n)\) (Turán’s Theorem).
